Worship's natural companion is truth. The quest for truth runs like a current through every ancient text, whether biblical, Qur'anic, philosophical or psychological.

## What is truth?

A collection of facts? Reality as it is? The architecture of the cosmos? Or the ultimate horizon of human existence? Pilate asked it aloud at Jesus’ trial: "What is truth?" (John 18:38)

### The Bible and the Abrahamic traditions

In Scripture, truth is relational and rooted in being. It is carried by the Hebrew *emet* (firmness, faithfulness, reliability) and the Greek *aletheia* (that which is un-hidden, revealed). In the Gospel of John, Jesus says: "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:6), anchoring truth not merely in a statement but in a Person and a lived experience. In Islam, *al-Haqq*, the Truth or the Real, is one of the names of God, and the Qur'an says: "Truth hath come and falsehood hath vanished away." (Qur'an 17:81)

### The Eastern traditions

- **Hinduism:** *satya*, truth, is bound up with Brahman, ultimate reality. The Mundaka Upanishad proclaims *satyam eva jayate*: "The true prevails, not the untrue; by the true the path is laid out." (Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.6)
- **Buddhism:** truth is found in the Dharma and the Four Noble Truths: the plain facts of suffering, its cause, its ending, and the path that leads there. Here the veil is *avidya*, ignorance, and seeing through it means seeing the three marks of existence: impermanence, unsatisfactoriness and not-self.
- **Taoism:** the Tao is the unnameable reality beneath all things. "The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao." (Tao Te Ching 1) To live truly is to move in effortless alignment (*wu wei*) with the natural flow of the world.

### Philosophy and psychology

Classical philosophy defines truth by correspondence (matching reality) and coherence (holding together without contradiction). Aristotle put the first in one line: to say of what is that it is, and of what is not that it is not, is true. Carl Jung and depth psychology see psychological truth as integration: bringing the hidden "shadow" into awareness so that a person can become whole.

## The pillars of truth

If truth is a temple, what keeps it standing?

- **Correspondence with reality.** Truth does not bend to what we want; it describes what is actually there, in the physical world and the moral one. A bridge built on wishful sums falls down no matter how sincerely the engineer believed them; a lie told to a friend corrodes the friendship whether or not it is ever found out.
- **Lived coherence.** In both biblical wisdom and Confucian thought (*cheng*, sincerity), truth cannot be separated from action. Jesus saved his sharpest words for people whose religion was all outside: "Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye pay tithe of mint and anise and cummin, and have omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith." (Matthew 23:23) They were "like unto whited sepulchres, which indeed appear beautiful outward, but are within full of dead men’s bones" (Matthew 23:27). A truth that does not change how one lives is incomplete, even dead.
- **Universality.** Cultural expressions differ, but core truths about human dignity, justice, compassion and the cost of deception appear in every civilization.
- **A transcendent anchor.** Across the great spiritual traditions, truth is anchored in something higher than human agreement: a divine order, a cosmic law, an absolute reality.

## Why are we drawn to truth?

We need truth the way the body needs oxygen. For the Bible's readers, we are made in the image of a God whose word "is true from the beginning" (Psalm 119:160), and the soul longs for its source. The Eastern traditions say something similar from another angle: for Hindu Vedanta, waking up means stripping away the veil of *maya*, illusion, to remember our true nature; for Buddhism, it means seeing through ignorance. One movement goes from outside in, the other from inside out, but both are a homecoming.

And people live by meaning, which cannot be built on falsehood. Deception brings inner conflict, anxiety and decay; truth brings peace and alignment.

## What is the opposite of truth?

The opposite of truth is not just a factual mistake. It is deception and distortion: something that wears the mask of reality while draining the life from those who trust it.

### The parable of the painted well

Two travelers crossed an endless desert under a relentless sun. Halfway across they came upon a grand courtyard built around a stone well. Banners hung from the pillars: *Here lies the water of life.* Overjoyed, the first traveler lowered his bucket and pulled up dry dust. Looking closer, he saw that the sparkling blue water at the bottom was painted onto flat stone, and the cool breeze came from hidden bellows.

The keeper of the courtyard smiled and said: "Why walk further in the heat? Stay here, admire the painting, and tell yourself you are refreshed." The first traveler walked back into the sun to keep searching. The second stayed behind, staring at the painted water, until thirst claimed him.

Deception rarely announces itself as poison. It imitates the shape of what the soul craves, offering the look of nourishment without the substance that sustains life.

## Do we all believe in truth?

Yes, whatever our religion or definition. Even the radical skeptic presupposes truth when arguing that skepticism is true. The disagreement is rarely over whether truth exists, but over where the authority to define it lies: divine revelation, science, personal intuition or tradition. The very act of arguing over right and wrong shows that we share the assumption of a real standard.

## Truth for believers and for non-believers

- **Believers.** Source: transcendent. Nature: absolute, unchanging, morally demanding. Authority: scripture, revelation, tradition and spiritual experience.
- **Non-believers.** Source: observation, reasoning and shared evidence. Nature: discovered, growing (in science and in society), practical. Authority: the scientific method, peer review, human reason and ethical humanism.

Both share a deep reverence for honesty, integrity and the pursuit of reality, the things that free people from ignorance.

## Does truth change over time?

Truth itself, as ultimate reality, does not change. But our grasp of it and our words for it grow.

- **Revelation unfolding.** In biblical theology, God reveals truth step by step as humanity matures: from the covenants with the patriarchs, to the law, the prophets, and, for Christians, the Messiah.
- **Science and culture.** Our understanding of the cosmos deepens over centuries. What once seemed impossible becomes known, enlarging our sense of the universe's complexity without changing the laws that govern it.

## Where does truth lead?

Across sacred texts and human experience, truth is never meant to stay an idea. It has a destination:

- **Freedom.** "And ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free." (John 8:32) Truth breaks the chains of addiction, manipulation and self-deception.
- **Liberation.** In the Eastern traditions, seeing reality as it is, without clinging, ends the cycle of suffering: *moksha* in Hinduism, *nirvana* in Buddhism.
- **Wholeness.** Psychological truth brings integration, quiets inner division, and lets a person live authentically.

## The cost of truth

History is shaped by those who valued truth more than comfort.

- **Illusion and ego.** The first sacrifice truth asks is the comfort of being right, our favorite biases, and fitting in.
- **Safety and status.** Prophets, scientists and martyrs, from Socrates and Jesus to Gandhi and modern whistleblowers, gave up standing, freedom and even life, because living a lie seemed worse than death.

As Scripture puts it: "Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding." (Proverbs 23:23) That remains the great vocation of the human journey.

## Reflection

- Where in your life are you tempted by a "painted well": something that looks like what you need but isn't?
- Is there a truth you agree with but haven't yet let change how you live?
- What has telling the truth cost you, and was it worth it?
