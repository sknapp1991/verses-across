## Two ways of knowing

Artificial intelligence now writes fluent prose, paints striking pictures and produces working code. It is natural to ask: *what kind of knowing is this, and how does it compare to ours?* Two pictures help. One is a modern parable, the **Blind Sculptor**. The other is ancient: Adam naming the animals, in the Bible and the Qur'an.

## The Blind Sculptor

Imagine an artisan shut in a pitch-black, soundproof room with a block of marble, a hammer and a chisel. He has never seen the sun, never met a living creature, and has no idea what a horse is.

Outside, overseers watching through a peephole want a marble horse. They cannot speak to him. They can only answer each blow with a chime: low when it misses, high when it comes closer.

He strikes almost at random, again and again: billions, trillions of times, guided only by the chime. Flakes of stone fall in the dark. Slowly a neck appears, then legs, shoulders, nostrils, a mane. When the room is opened, there stands a flawless marble stallion, every tendon in place, and the world says: *"The sculptor knows what a horse is!"*

But he does not. He has never felt life, never ridden across a plain. His "knowledge" is the gathered shadow of billions of corrections from outside. He made the likeness from the **outside in**.

## How machines learn

This is, in outline, how today's AI is made. A neural network starts as random numbers (the marble). It compares its output with what people wrote or drew (the overseers' world), measures the error (the chime), and nudges its numbers by tiny amounts, an enormous number of times (gradient descent), then is refined by human ratings. Each nudge is calculated rather than guessed, but it only ever sees the error, never the horse.

It masters the *patterns* of human language and art. It can write a sonnet or picture a horse in a moonlit field, not because it knows love or horses, but because it has carved away everything that does not match what people made. And what counts as right always comes from outside the box: the human-made data it is scored against, and people's ratings.

## Adam names the animals

The scriptures describe the opposite: knowing from the **inside out**.

In Genesis, God brings the animals "unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof." (Genesis 2:19)

In the Qur'an, God tells the angels He will place a viceroy on the earth, and they object: "Wilt Thou place therein one who will do harm therein and will shed blood, while we, we hymn Thy praise and sanctify Thee?" God answers: "Surely I know that which ye know not." (Qur'an 2:30)

Then "He taught Adam all the names", and asked the angels to name the creatures. They could not: "We have no knowledge saving that which Thou hast taught us." (Qur'an 2:31–32) Adam could. No billion blows, no chime, no guessing. In this essay's reading, each name voiced what the creature truly is.

## Why Adam could

Man was not made as a hollow machine shaped from outside. "And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul." (Genesis 2:7) The Qur'an: "when I have made him and have breathed into him of My spirit". (Qur'an 15:29)

Genesis also says "God created man in his own image" (Genesis 1:27), what Christians call the *imago Dei*. Made by the same Maker as the animals and carrying that image, Adam recognizes a horse from within: its strength, its freedom, its life. His naming is not trial and error. It is being recognizing being.

## Side by side

- **Direction:** the machine carves inward from noise; Adam knows outward from the breath of God.
- **Method:** endless correction, or direct recognition.
- **Source of truth:** correction from outside, or an inner life.
- **Result:** a hollow likeness, or a living name.

## The angels' mistake, and ours

The angels judged humanity from the outside: clay, hunger, violence. God saw what they could not measure: the capacity to reflect Him from within, to love, worship and give meaning.

In building AI we are making something in our own image. But we cannot breathe the breath of God into silicon, so we build the Blind Sculptor's way. The real danger is not that machines will become conscious. It is that we will forget the difference between the **marble horse** and the **living stallion**, and shrink our idea of the soul down to statistics.

We can admire the statue and use it well, in medicine, science and art. But the machine does not feel the sun, does not weep, does not pray, and does not know the horse. Our dignity rests on the breath of God within us.

> "Who teacheth by the pen, Teacheth man that which he knew not." (Qur'an 96:4–5)

## Reflection

- Where have you mistaken a perfect likeness for the real thing?
- What do you know from the inside, by living it, that no amount of information could have taught you?
- What is one thing in your day you could truly "name": give your full attention, rather than only process?
