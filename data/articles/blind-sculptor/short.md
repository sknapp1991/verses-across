## The sculptor in the dark

Picture a sculptor shut in a pitch-black room with a block of marble. He has never seen a horse. Outside, people who want a marble horse can only answer each blow with a chime: closer, or further. After billions of blows a perfect stallion stands in the dark, every tendon in place, and the world says: "He knows what a horse is!"

He doesn't. He shaped its likeness from the **outside in**, one correction at a time. That, in outline, is how artificial intelligence learns: it matches the patterns of what people made, guided by measures of its error, without ever meeting the horse.

## Adam in the garden

The scriptures tell of the opposite way of knowing. God "brought them unto Adam to see what he would call them: and whatsoever Adam called every living creature, that was the name thereof." (Genesis 2:19) In the Qur'an the angels could not name the creatures ("We have no knowledge saving that which Thou hast taught us"), but Adam could. (Qur'an 2:31–33)

He knew from the **inside out**, because "the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul." (Genesis 2:7)

## The danger

The risk of the AI age is not that machines will wake up. It is that we will forget the difference between the marble horse and the living stallion. The machine does not feel the sun, weep, pray or know the horse. Our dignity rests on the breath of God within us.

## Reflection

- Where have you mistaken a perfect likeness for the real thing?
- What do you know from the inside, by living it, that no amount of information could have taught you?
- What is one thing in your day you could truly "name": give your full attention, rather than only process?
