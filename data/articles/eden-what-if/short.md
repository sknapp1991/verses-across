## The two trees

The Eden story turns on two trees: the tree of life and the tree of knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 2:9). Most theology dwells on what happened: the wrong choice, then exile. But imagining other orders of events reveals a lot about freedom, conscience, morality and death. (The sayings and parables below are the essay's own.)

## Path one: only the tree of life

Here humanity eats only from the tree of life and never touches the tree of knowledge. People live on and on, innocent and untroubled.

Without knowing good and evil, though, moral choice is impossible. Humanity would be like a well-kept garden: harmonious, unchanging, but without real self-determination. There would be no suffering, but also no triumph, no heroism, and none of the hard testing that forms character.

*A river frozen in crystal cannot turn a mill wheel, and a soul that never wakes cannot write a song of redemption.*

**Parable: the ageless child.** A king built a great orchard for his son, but locked the door of the library. The son lived a thousand summers and never aged. Yet when the king asked him what justice was, he only smiled and pointed at the lilies. He had all the time in the world, but not one moment of real choice.

## Path two: life first, then knowledge

Here Adam and Eve eat from the tree of life first, and only then from the tree of knowledge.

Having gained immortality before conscience, humanity falls into moral struggle with a body that cannot die. The consequences of rebellion, death and decay, are cancelled in advance. Guilt, remorse and moral struggle go on forever, with no end to bring rest.

## Path three: knowledge first, then life

Here humanity first gains the knowledge of good and evil, with all its pain, and only later receives the tree of life. The Bible guards against taking it too soon, while still fallen: "lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever" (Genesis 3:22). So the way to the tree was guarded, and the gift was kept for later.

On this path, people become self-aware, feel their nakedness and weakness, and pass through history, labor and hard choices, and *then* receive eternal life. Immortality goes not to an innocent child but to a tested, scarred and wiser survivor.

*Gold tested twice in the furnace, crowned at last with a sun that never sets.*

**Parable: the broken vessel.** An apprentice dropped a masterpiece vase and it shattered on the stone floor. Weeping, he gathered the pieces and mended every seam with liquid gold, until the vase was stronger than before, shining at every crack. Only then did the master give him the drink of unending days. The vessel would last forever, not because it had never fallen, but because its breaking had taught it how to hold the infinite.

## What the three paths show

Path one keeps peace at the cost of awareness. Path two risks endless inner torment in an undying body. Path three traces the whole arc of the spiritual journey: from unconscious innocence, through the testing of choice, into a conscious life that lasts forever. And the Bible's last book promises exactly that: "Blessed are they that do his commandments, that they may have right to the tree of life" (Revelation 22:14).

## Reflection

- Would you choose safety without growth, if it were offered?
- Which "crack" in your own life has been mended with gold?
