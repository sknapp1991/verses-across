## Everyone kneels somewhere

We usually picture worship as something that happens in a building: songs, prayers, a bowed head. Underneath every form, it is simpler than that. To worship something is to give it your **attention**, your **love** and your **effort**, and to treat it as the thing that matters most.

Seen that way, the question is not *whether* we worship, but *what*. If we don't choose, something will choose for us: money, approval, fear, comfort or plain habit will happily take the empty seat.

## Good things, ultimate things

Most of what pulls on a human life is good: safety, love, work, beauty, being respected. The trouble begins when a good thing is asked to be the ultimate thing. Jesus put it in one line: "For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also." (Matthew 6:21)

The traditions agree on the danger. The first commandment is "Thou shalt have no other gods before me." (Exodus 20:3) The Qur'an's opening prayer says "Thee (alone) we worship; Thee (alone) we ask for help." (1:5) In the Bhagavad Gita, Krishna asks for nothing grand, only devotion: "a leaf, a flower, a fruit, water" offered with a whole heart (9:26).

## We become like what we worship

The Psalms say it bluntly about idols of silver and gold: "They that make them are like unto them; so is every one that trusteth in them." (Psalm 115:8)

A finite object of worship (money, status, our own image) can be lost, so it keeps us anxious and guarded. It makes a life smaller. Worship of what the traditions call the infinite tends to do the opposite: it makes a life larger, more generous and less afraid, because what it loves cannot be taken away.

## The test

Words are cheap, so words are a poor measure. The real test comes when your altar asks for a sacrifice:

**What are you willing to lose rather than betray what you revere?**

- If you say you worship God but cut corners, withhold mercy or hoard out of fear, your real altar may be your own safety.
- If you say you worship truth or human dignity but bend the facts or abandon a friend to keep your standing, your real altar may be comfort or status.

The rule holds for believer and unbeliever alike: **your true object of worship is whatever you refuse to sacrifice for anything else.**

## How then shall we worship?

The prophet Micah answered long ago: "He hath shewed thee, O man, what is good; and what doth the LORD require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?" (Micah 6:8)

## Reflection

- What do you protect first when you feel threatened?
- What would you give up before you gave that up?

The answer is your altar. Choose it on purpose, then watch what it makes of you.
